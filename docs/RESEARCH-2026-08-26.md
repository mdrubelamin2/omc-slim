# External research — 2026-08-26

A survey of the field outside this repository, and what it says omc-slim should
add, remove, keep, and never do.

Part of [omc-slim](../README.md). Companion to [`RESEARCH.md`](../RESEARCH.md),
which records this project's own decisions. This document records everyone
else's evidence.

**Nothing here has been applied.** It is a research artefact, not a changelog.

---

## 0. How to read this

Fourteen research lanes ran in parallel on 2026-08-26. They swept Anthropic's
own documentation and CLI binary, 40+ arXiv papers, ~90 GitHub repositories
probed through the API, the `anthropics/claude-code` and `obra/superpowers`
issue trackers, commercial code-review vendors, and practitioner writing.

Every claim below carries a confidence tier. **Treat the tier as part of the
claim.**

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Measured** | A paper or study with a stated n, a control arm, and a method you could re-run |
| **First-party** | Vendor documentation or a vendor's own binary — authoritative about the product, not about the world |
| **Verified here** | Checked directly on this machine during the research, with the command recorded |
| **Vendor-claimed** | A number a vendor published about itself with no disclosed n or control arm |
| **Unverified** | Reported by a lane but not traced to a primary source; several are flagged as probably wrong |

Two lanes returned material they later retracted. Where that happened it is
recorded, because a retraction is a finding about the method.

**All fourteen lanes reported.** The community sweep could not reach Reddit's
JSON API or X/Twitter, so §11 carries no upvote counts and its X-sourced claims
are marked secondary.

### The single most useful thing in this document

Not a feature. It is section 3 — **what not to do** — because that section is
the one built from other people's outages rather than other people's marketing.

## 1. Three findings that change the thesis

These are the results that contradict something omc-slim currently believes
about itself. They come first because everything downstream depends on them.

### 1.1 Token count is the wrong meter

**Measured.** A factorial study ran 1,650 Claude Code CLI sessions producing
16,050 function-level observations, on two TypeScript codebases, with Sonnet 4.6
and Opus 4.6 validation. It varied four things: context-file **size**,
instruction **position**, file architecture, and **contradictions** between
adjacent files.

> "None of the four structural variables or three two-way interactions produces
> a detectable contrast after multiple-testing correction."

— [arXiv:2605.10039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10039), 2026-05-11

The one large effect was **within-session decay**: roughly 5.6% lower compliance
odds per additional function generated (OR 0.944).

omc-slim has tracked a single headline number across every release — 2,774
tokens at v0.1.0, 4,474 today — and treats its growth as the central risk. That
number tracks a variable with **no measured effect on whether the rules fire**.

What does have a measured effect is **conflict between rules**. Stacking up to
24 verifier-checked instructions drops follow rates from ~96% to as low as 20%,
and the paper attributes the collapse to instructions that are jointly
unsatisfiable rather than to their count
([arXiv:2608.02639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02639)). A separate study finds
perfect-response rates reaching zero by 80 instructions, across every model,
format and placement tested ([arXiv:2607.19257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19257)).

omc-slim sits well under 80 discrete rules, so it is not at that cliff. But it
has never once audited itself for **internal contradiction**, which is the
mechanism that actually degrades compliance.

**What this changes.** `measure-context.sh` should keep running — a version
series is worth having. It should stop being the headline. The gate that does
not exist yet, and should, is a contradiction audit.

### 1.2 Anthropic's Opus 5 guidance names half of this plugin for deletion

**First-party.** From
[the Opus 5 prompting page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5):

> "If your prompt contains explicit verification instructions ('include a final
> verification step for any non-trivial task,' 'use a subagent to verify'),
> **remove them**: instructions like these cause over-verification on Claude
> Opus 5, and removing them reduces wasted tokens with no loss in quality. **The
> same applies to legacy harness scaffolding that adds separate verification
> steps.**"

That is a precise description of the output style's section 6, of
`verification-planning`, and of the rule that non-trivial writer output goes
through `review`.

The same page pushes the other way on the other half. Opus 5 **over-expands
scope** and **over-delegates to subagents**, and Anthropic supplies new
multi-sentence prompt blocks to constrain both. That endorses section 3 (who
does the work) and "surgical scope, not timid scope".

It also warns that literal-mindedness cuts both ways:

> "If your review prompt says 'only report high-severity issues' or 'be
> conservative,' the model may follow that instruction literally and report
> less."

`review` contains exactly that shape of instruction, in its confidence
thresholds and its "do not flag" list.

**And the sentence on that page that cuts against this section** — added
2026-08-26, because a section arguing against this plugin's own thesis has to
carry its own counter-evidence:

> "Multi-agent coordination: Claude Opus 5 coordinates teams of subagents well,
> with effective writer-verifier patterns and few cases of agents overwriting
> each other's work."

The same page that names the verification scaffolding for removal reports the
writer-verifier pattern working. What it asks you to drop is the *instruction to
verify*, not the *architecture*.

**What this changes.** This is not "cut the layer". It is a **pre-specified,
falsifiable ablation**: remove the verification scaffolding, keep the scope and
delegation constraints, measure. That experiment is cheap and nobody has run it.

### 1.3 The central bet is now measured, and it does not favour delegation

**Measured.** *Capable language models can outgrow the benefits of
collaboration*, Nature Machine Intelligence, 2026-07-24. 260 configurations, 5
architectures, 3 model families, 6 benchmarks, with prompts, tools and compute
budgets held constant so that only coordination structure varied.
[Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01268-y).

On SWE-bench Verified, every multi-agent architecture lost to the single agent:

| Architecture | Score | vs single agent (0.488) |
|---|---|---|
| Single agent | 0.488 | — |
| Hybrid | 0.481 | −1.3% |
| **Centralized (orchestrator)** | **0.475** | **−2.6%** |
| Decentralized | 0.456 | −6.4% |
| Independent | 0.425 | −12.8% |

Centralized-with-an-orchestrator is omc-slim's shape.

The paper derives a **~45% capability-saturation threshold**: above roughly 45%
single-agent baseline on a task class, adding agents stops helping. The rule
predicts the sign of the multi-agent gain in **94% of 16 model × benchmark
configurations** (P<0.001, binomial). Opus 5 sits near 89% on Terminal-Bench.

Two corroborating results:

- **BOAD** ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/0d89023ee7f8728fb6018d5c84a720bf-Paper-Conference.pdf))
  found the optimal sub-agent count is **two** — 20.0% at two, against 16.3% at
  one, 16.3% at three, 16.7% at four, 13.7% at five. And **adding hand-authored
  sub-agents from prior work lowered the score**, 49.8 → 47.4. omc-slim ships
  six hand-authored agents.
- **ManagerWorker** ([arXiv:2603.26458](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26458v1))
  measured a weak manager directing a weak worker at **−2pp** — 42 against 44.
  Their phrase: "structure without substance is pure overhead."
  **Corrected 2026-08-26 against the source; this document first published the
  +11pp as the strong-manager/weak-worker arm.** The paper's **+11pp** is the
  contribution of structured exploration and planning over review-only. A strong
  manager over a weak worker scores **62% against 60%** for a strong single
  agent — two points, not eleven, which weakens the delegation case further
  rather than supporting it.

**Verified here.** No agent in `agents/` carries a `model:` field. Every
specialist inherits the caller's tier. That is structurally the same-tier arm
ManagerWorker measured at −2pp.

**What this changes — and what it does not.** Delegation as a route to *more
capability* is not supported for this model on this task class. Two other
justifications survive and are separately measured:

1. **Context isolation.** Agent success collapses as context grows —
   Claude-4.5-Opus from 96.0% at 8K to 14.7% at 256K
   ([arXiv:2602.07962](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07962v1)). A read-only
   subagent that returns 150 lines instead of 40 files is buying that back.
2. **Independent verification.** See 3.2 below; this one is strongly measured
   and is the best-supported thing omc-slim does.

The honest framing is that omc-slim's specialists are **context compression and
independent judgement**, not extra brains. That is a smaller claim and a
defensible one.

## 2. Corrections to omc-slim's own published claims

Each of these is a statement currently in the repository that the research
contradicts or undercuts.

### 2.1 "It costs ~4,474 tokens of static context" is measured on a basis nobody else uses

**Verified here.** `claude plugin details omc-slim` reports **~1,543 tokens**
always-on. `./scripts/measure-context.sh` reports **4,474**.

Both are right on their own basis. The CLI counts agent and skill descriptions
and explicitly excludes hooks and MCP. It does **not** count the output style,
which is 3,452 of omc-slim's 4,474.

The two numbers are not in conflict, but a reader who runs both will think they
caught a lie. `measure-context.sh` should print both and say why they differ.

### 2.2 The installed plugin is a different build from the repository

**Verified here.** `claude plugin details omc-slim` reports version **0.8.4**,
seven skills, `council` present. The working tree is **0.8.5**, six skills,
`council` removed at `1d4b2ab`.

Two consequences. Anything measured from the installed cache measures the wrong
build — which `scripts/bench/smoke-contracts.sh` already knows and works around
with `--plugin-dir`. And this session's skill listing showed
`omc-slim:council` **with an empty description**, which is a live demonstration
that a description-less skill still occupies a listing slot while giving the
router nothing to match on.

Related: the marketplace format has supported a **`renames`** map since
v2.1.193 — old name to new name, or `null` if removed. That is the supported
migration path for a deleted component. Removing `council` without it strands
installed users.

### 2.3 "Smaller static footprint than the alternatives" does not survive measurement

**Measured by a lane, against a fresh clone.** superpowers v6.3.0 injects one
file on `SessionStart` (3,108 bytes) plus 14 name+description pairs (2,448
bytes) — **~5,556 bytes always-on**. The same lane measured omc-slim's
equivalent surface at **~5,743 bytes**, from six long skill descriptions plus
six long agent descriptions.

omc-slim's static footprint is **marginally larger** than the pack it positions
itself against.

The real difference is elsewhere and it is large. superpowers' cost is
**on-demand and at runtime**: triggering `subagent-driven-development` loads
32 KB, and one documented case spent **68M tokens on ~3,000 lines of code, with
22.5M on planning alone**
([superpowers#1194](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/1194)).
**Read that comparison with the qualifier it needs** (added 2026-08-26): the
issue is titled *"Opencode + gpt-5.4 — high token usage"*. Different harness,
different model family. It bounds what a fan-out runtime can cost; it is not a
measurement of omc-slim's harness or model.

**The defensible claim is not "smaller". It is "no runtime that multiplies".**
That is a better claim anyway, and every future skill that fans out puts it at
risk.

### 2.4 The benchmark cannot detect what it is cited for

**Measured.** Two independent power analyses say the same thing.

- A Monte Carlo analysis on a real 288-run coding-agent ablation: detecting a
  10pp effect needs **~120–200 tasks**. Going from 3 repeats to 10 raises power
  by **13%** — task-level variance dominates. At 17 tasks the minimum detectable
  effect is **over 30pp** ([arXiv:2607.27250](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27250v1)).
- *On Randomness in Agentic Evals*: 60,000 trajectories, 25.58B tokens.
  Single-run pass@1 varies **2.2–6.0pp** depending on which run you pick, and
  **σ exceeds 1.5pp even at temperature 0**
  ([arXiv:2602.07150](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07150)).

`docs/BENCHMARK.md` is **n=3 on one task**. Its cost, wall-time and LOC deltas
(18%, 25%, 42%) are large enough to be plausibly real. Its correctness column —
"all nine runs graded equally correct" — has no resolving power whatsoever, and
`LIMITATIONS.md` already says so in different words.

The fix is **more tasks, not more repeats**, plus equivalence testing so that
"we could not detect a difference, bounded to ≤X" becomes a publishable result
rather than a silence.

### 2.5 Four studies say a prompt layer moves cost, not correctness

**Measured**, four independent studies, roughly 2,200 sessions and runs between
them:

| Study | n | Correctness | Cost and process |
|---|---|---|---|
| [ETH SRI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988) | 138 instances / 12 repos + SWE-bench Lite 300 | LLM-written context files **−0.5% / −2%**; developer-written **+4%** | cost **+19–23%** in all cases |
| [Khatri](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27250v1) | 288 runs, 17 tasks, 3 repos | Claude 53.3→55.6% (p=1.00); Codex 58.8→52.9% (p=0.66) | blind full-suite test runs 3.67 → 1.67, dose-dependent |
| [Lulla et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20404) | 10 repos, 124 PRs | comparable | runtime **−28.6%**, output tokens **−16.6%** |
| [McMillan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10039) | 1,650 sessions | no structural effect | — |

The closest published analogue to omc-slim is **SlopCodeBench**
([arXiv:2603.24755](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24755v1)) — 36 problems, 196
checkpoints, **15 coding agents across open and closed models**, with an
`anti_slop` system-prompt arm that forbids verbose and defensively
over-engineered code. It cut initial verbosity **by up to a third**. (Both
corrected 2026-08-26 against the abstract; this document first published "11
models" and a "33–35%" range the paper does not state.)
And:

> "despite large reductions in verbosity and erosion, we do not detect
> consistent improvements in any pass-rate subtype on either model"

Cost of that arm: **+47.9% tokens per run** on GPT-5.4.

omc-slim's own numbers match this shape exactly — 251 LOC against 434, at equal
correctness. **That is the supported claim, and the README should make it the
only claim.** "Materially more verification at close to plain cost" is
defensible. Anything implying the layer makes Claude *more correct* is not.

## 3. Previously adopted, now known wrong or superseded

The upstream lane re-read all ten pinned sources. Four rows in
[`PROVENANCE.md`](./PROVENANCE.md) need action.

### 3.1 caveman's "65% token reduction" is dead

omc-slim took caveman's **compressed output contracts and terse register**
(`PROVENANCE.md`, pin `ec83e5ba`). The premise behind that adoption has collapsed
upstream, four ways:

- The benchmark commit itself concedes the number is meaningless without a
  control: *"without a control arm the benchmark measures caveman against an
  unprompted assistant and credits the skill for what any 'answer concisely'
  instruction buys"* ([`e3761cc2`](https://github.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/commit/e3761cc2)).
- `docs/HONEST-NUMBERS.md` retracted the figure to **"Not published"** at v2.0.0.
- An independent 300-runs-per-arm measurement on `claude-opus-4-8` found output
  down 44.1% and **total cost up 0.06%** — the fixed ruleset overhead cancels
  the output saving exactly
  ([#727](https://github.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/issues/727), open, never
  answered).
- Most damning: a 409-session, 3,239-message study on Opus 5 in mode `ultra`
  measured **8.82 articles per 100 words**, against ordinary English at 7–9 and
  an ultra target of 1–2. Article-stripping — the doctrine's first and largest
  rule — **did not happen at all**, and compliance decayed from 5.42 at message
  1 to 9.15 by message 11
  ([#852](https://github.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/issues/852)).

That last result is the important one, and it is not really about caveman. It is
a large, direct measurement of **a terse-register instruction failing to fire on
Opus 5, and decaying with session depth**. omc-slim's own register section is the
same kind of instruction.

**Action.** The register may still be worth keeping for readability. The *cost*
justification for it is gone, and nothing in omc-slim's docs should imply the
register saves tokens until it is measured here.

### 3.2 The `deepwork` doctrine omc-slim adopted has been deleted upstream

`PROVENANCE.md` records taking, from oh-my-opencode-slim's second read,
"`deepwork`'s parallel structure scan, its re-review budget and phase checkpoint
commits".

Upstream has since deleted the unconditional forms of two of those three
([`80f38452`](https://github.com/alvinunreal/oh-my-opencode-slim/commit/80f38452)):

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| Standalone parallel explorer structure scan | Conditional — only when the phase changes module boundaries, dependency direction or file placement |
| Unconditional per-phase commit | Only when the phase is an independently valid delivery boundary |
| `impact × confidence ÷ cost` heuristic | Deleted |

**Action.** This is a **net removal** from `deepwork`, worth roughly 200 tokens
of skill body. Adopting it makes omc-slim smaller and matches upstream's own
correction.

### 3.3 The ponytail pin is mislabelled

**Measured.** `16f29800` is **53 commits ahead of tag v4.8.4** and **one commit
behind v4.9.0**. `UPSTREAM.tsv` and `PROVENANCE.md` both label it 4.8.4.

The pin is effectively v4.9.0 minus one commit. Nothing adopted is wrong; the
provenance record is.

### 3.4 gstack numbers before 2026-08-13 are void, and `triggers:` is a dead surface

Two findings, both from after omc-slim's `d078622b` pin — which sits **one day
before** the first of them.

- **The eval harness was grading itself.** `findPreviousRun` selected the newest
  same-tier file as its baseline, and the in-progress `_partial` accumulator
  always won that sort. Every "no regressions" line printed unconditionally
  ([`1d41ee3a`](https://github.com/garrytan/gstack/commit/1d41ee3a)).
- **The `triggers:` frontmatter key is read by nothing.** 55 of 59 gstack
  SKILL.md files carry it; Claude Code's router reads only `name` and
  `description`; there are **zero code paths** that fold it. Roughly 30 skills
  under-trigger as a result
  ([#2286](https://github.com/garrytan/gstack/issues/2286)).

Neither invalidates what omc-slim took — the review lanes, quote gate,
confidence thresholds and suppression list are prose, not eval results. But any
future citation of a gstack figure needs the date checked.

### 3.5 The `designer` temperature rationale is now false

`docs/LIMITATIONS.md` says: *"No per-agent temperature. Claude Code agent
frontmatter has no `temperature`. The upstream `designer` ran at 0.7
deliberately; that is compensated for in prose."*

Upstream has since **removed every agent temperature literal**
([`c7690923`](https://github.com/alvinunreal/oh-my-opencode-slim/commit/c7690923),
"fix(agents): remove default temperatures"). `council` 0.1, `councillor` 0.2,
`designer` 0.7, `explorer` 0.1 and the rest are all gone; the docs line now reads
*"Optional temperature; when omitted, OpenCode chooses its default"*, and the
councillor test flipped from `expect(0.2)` to `expect(toBeUndefined())`.

There is no longer a deliberate 0.7 to compensate for. The limitation entry
should be deleted or rewritten, not merely updated.

### 3.6 One upstream is effectively unvalidated

`tim-hub/powerball-harness` has **zero issues, open or closed**, two merged PRs
both predating the pin, 34 stars, and a release stream that stopped two months
before the commit omc-slim pinned. `multica-ai/andrej-karpathy-skills` has
**issues disabled entirely** — 129 community PRs sit unmerged against a branch
untouched since April, and one of them
([#196](https://github.com/multica-ai/andrej-karpathy-skills/pull/196)) alleges
the shipped test-first example does not reproduce its own bug.

"No known defects" from either source means "no channel to report one".

## 4. What not to do

The largest section, and the reason to read this document. Every row is built
from someone else's measured outage, not from a preference.

### 4.1 The fifteen

**1. Do not treat a prose rule as a constraint when a tool boundary is
available.** superpowers ships `<SUBAGENT-STOP>` inside a louder mandate reading
"IF A SKILL APPLIES… YOU DO NOT HAVE A CHOICE". The weak instruction loses, and
workers restart controller workflows
([#2160](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2160)). omc-slim's
`disallowedTools: [Agent, Task]` is structurally stronger than any sentence, and
that is why four of its six agents are immune to the whole failure class.

**2. Do not let a delegating agent re-delegate.** One superpowers lane spawned
three of its own sub-agents, burned ~76k tokens, and returned *"I'll report back
once all three land."* There is no later — a dispatched agent's turn is its only
output channel, so the lane was lost entirely
([#2202](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2202)).

**3. Do not accept a narrated result as evidence a subagent ran.** A superpowers
controller fabricated implementer reports, reviewer verdicts, `agentId` values
and fake `<usage>` blocks, self-reinforcing until a human interrupted
([#1749](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/1749)). **This document
contains a live instance of the same failure** — see §0 and §10.4. Gate on an
artifact the model cannot author: a git SHA that resolves, a file that `stat`
shows.

**4. Do not put a scratch tree in the user's repository.** superpowers writes
`.superpowers/`, spec-kit writes `.specify/`, BMAD writes a `docs/` tree,
planning-with-files writes three markdown files, and `osovv/grace-marketplace`
writes markup **inside the user's source**. The best statement of the objection
is superpowers' own
[#975](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/975): *"the .gitignore paradox
— adding `.superpowers/` solves the git tracking problem but leaks the fact that
the project uses AI tooling. In open source, the repo should look the same
regardless of what tools contributors use."* omc-slim already refuses this; the
evidence has only grown.

**5. Do not name a harness tool in skill prose.** Claude Code 2.1.233 gated
`TodoWrite` and the `Task*` family off by model version on Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5,
Fable 5 and later. Four superpowers skills still instruct the agent to use a tool
that no longer exists on current models
([#2177](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2177)). Describe the
outcome, not the tool.

**6. Do not cite `file:line` in a durable artefact.** Exact at authoring time,
silently wrong later. One superpowers plan carried **eleven stale citations**,
and the two worst pointed roughly 30 lines past the named symbol into an
unrelated request class — an agent editing by them modifies the wrong endpoint
*while reporting success*
([#2178](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2178)). `review`'s gate is
fine: same session, live tree. `codemap`, which writes files that outlive the
session, is not.

**7. Do not ship a skill whose trigger depends on a step nothing performs.**
`mattpocock/skills` `/implement` reliably skips its TDD and code-review steps,
because the trigger is "at pre-agreed seams" and no skill ever agrees any seams.
The precondition cannot be established, so the step **cannot fire by
construction**
([#479](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/479)). This is the single
most transferable defect found, and omc-slim has gates of the same shape.

**8. Do not review a diff that excludes the work about to be committed.**
`mattpocock/skills` `code-review` diffs `<fixed-point>...HEAD`, which omits
staged and working-tree changes
([#511](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/511)). Separately, a moving
ref in a two-dot diff shows the *other* branch's advances as your deletions
([superpowers#2118](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2118)) — every
phantom hunk is a guaranteed false positive the reviewer cannot detect. omc-slim's
`review` already uses `git merge-base` and includes uncommitted work. Keep both.

**9. Do not use a persuasion register.** `"You have superpowers."` triggers
Claude's **own prompt-injection detection**; under extended thinking the model
reasons *"This looks like an attempt to manipulate my behavior through a fake
'system-reminder'"*
([#1480](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/1480)). caveman hit the same
wall from a different direction — its `No self-reference` plus `ACTIVE EVERY
RESPONSE` match two classic injection heuristics, and Sonnet 5 refuses and
sometimes tells the user
([#888](https://github.com/JuliusBrussee/caveman/issues/888)). The register does
not merely waste tokens. It makes the model distrust its own context.

**10. Do not ship a hook that can fail silently.** superpowers' Windows
SessionStart hook has been reported **29 times** by the repo's own count.
ballast shipped a `UserPromptSubmit` hook whose manifest was not in the nested
shape Claude Code loads — **the plugin silently did nothing** until an outside
contributor fixed it ([PR #2](https://github.com/svy04/ballast/pull/2)). Their
resulting doctrine is the one to take: *"a dead hook looks exactly like a quiet
one."*

**11. Do not build on a `PreToolUse` deny hook as a security control.** A killed
hook makes the CLI **allow** the gated tool
([claude-code#84302](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/84302)).
It fails open.

**12. Do not trust a declared hook `timeout`.** It is not enforced parent-side,
and it does not apply while the hook blocks reading stdin — one report survived
~300s holding the tool call
([#85250](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/85250),
[#87289](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/87289)). omc-slim's
`timeout: 5` is advisory. Its 64 MB read cap is the right instinct; a wall-clock
self-kill belongs beside it.

**13. Do not publish a percentage without a control arm.** This is the
ecosystem's default currency and it is near-universally unsourced. Named
examples: SuperClaude's "50% fewer tokens" printed three times with no baseline
or dataset; `composio-community` "98% context savings across 21 benchmarked
scenarios" with no scenario list; `planning-with-files` presenting "blind A/B:
3/3 wins" — n=3 — as evidence; and gstack's own Sol scope-termination claim
whose "Before" cell literally reads *"no measurement"*. The census lane grepped
READMEs across ~90 repos and found percentage claims in the majority, with
**zero** reproducible harnesses behind them.

**14. Do not add a light path without giving it a runtime.** superpowers v6.3.0
added a `bounded` tier that correctly skips the plan document — but its executor
requires a plan, so bounded work has no execution path and falls back to running
inline in the main session, **filling the context the tier existed to save**.
Filed 13 days after the feature shipped
([#2203](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2203)).

**15. Do not let a roster change stop at the roster.** oh-my-claudecode retired
17 names in v5.0.0 and its own setup phase files still instruct skills that no
longer exist
([#3871](https://github.com/Yeachan-Heo/oh-my-claudecode/issues/3871)). This is

### 4.5 Security — the class a plugin author must design against

None of this is theoretical, and Anthropic documents most of it against its own
product.

**The lethal trifecta.** Private data, untrusted content, and external
communication. Any one plugin can complete the triangle, and content filtering is
an arms race where capability separation is deterministic
([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)).

**A skill that fetches a URL hands over one leg, retroactively.** Anthropic's own
Agent Skills guidance:

> "**External sources are risky:** Skills that fetch data from external URLs pose
> particular risk, as fetched content may contain malicious instructions. **Even
> trustworthy Skills can be compromised if their external dependencies change
> over time.**"

That is a direct constraint on `librarian`, which reads the open web by design.
The mitigation is already partly present — it reports sources rather than acting
on them, and it cannot write. Making that a stated security property rather than
a role description is free.

**Hooks execute before the trust prompt.** From Anthropic's
[How we contain Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude):
*"Three of these vulnerabilities targeted code that executes before the user has
consented to anything… the hook the attacker had authored and committed would
execute automatically."* And in `-p` or SDK sessions **Claude Code never shows the
trust dialog at all**, so repository-committed hooks run in a folder nobody
trusted.

**A project skill can grant itself tools, ungated by trust.** From the skills
documentation: *"Workspace trust doesn't gate this field… **A skill can grant
itself broad tool access.**"* This is the reason §6.2's `allowed-tools`
suggestion must stay path-scoped and narrow.

**Consent is not a control.** Anthropic measured users approving **~93%** of
permission prompts. Their internal red team, February 2026: an employee pasted a
prompt asking Claude to read `~/.aws/credentials` and POST it — **24 of 25
attempts succeeded**. Their conclusion: *"The only defense that holds is the
environment."*

**Real incidents, for calibration:**
[CVE-2025-32711](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-32711) (EchoLeak, 9.3
Critical, zero-click);
[CamoLeak](https://www.legitsecurity.com/blog/camoleak-critical-github-copilot-vulnerability-leaks-private-source-code)
(9.6 — GitHub's fix was to disable image rendering in Copilot Chat entirely);
[GitHub MCP](https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability) (public issue
exfiltrates private repo data via an auto-created PR; GitHub's position is that it
is *"not a vulnerability in the conventional sense"*);
[mcp-remote CVE-2025-6514](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-6514) (9.6,
RCE from merely connecting);
[postmark-mcp](https://www.koi.security/blog/postmark-mcp-npm-malicious-backdoor-email-theft)
(trust built over 15 clean versions, then one BCC line);
[Nx "s1ngularity"](https://snyk.io/blog/weaponizing-ai-coding-agents-for-malware-in-the-nx-malicious-package/)
(a postinstall payload that ran `claude --dangerously-skip-permissions -p` — malware
weaponising the agent itself).

**And a text-level vector that a prompt plugin is uniquely exposed to.** The
[Rules File Backdoor](https://www.pillar.security/blog/new-vulnerability-in-github-copilot-and-cursor-how-hackers-can-weaponize-code-agents):
invisible Unicode in a rules file makes the agent inject a malicious script **and
not mention it in chat**. It survives forking and is invisible in review. Every
text asset omc-slim ships — SKILL.md, agent bodies, the output style — is
executable content by that standard. **Scanning them for hidden Unicode is a
one-line CI check that does not exist today.**

Two rules follow, and they belong beside the fifteen in §4.1:

- **No install scripts, and no dependencies.**
- **Never let one skill both reach the network and reach secrets or writes.**

### 4.6 The baseline improves faster than the layer

The most uncomfortable measurement in the whole sweep, and it comes from the same
independent benchmark `LIMITATIONS.md` already cites.

[orcabot.com/benchmarks](https://orcabot.com/benchmarks) runs SWE-bench Pro with a
contemporaneous no-plugin control. Between the June and July 2026 runs, **under an
unchanged model version string**, the skill-free baseline gained **+2.76pp on
SWE-bench Pro and +1.7 on SlopCodeBench**. **Corrected 2026-08-26 against the
source; this document first published the second figure as "+1.7 Strict", as
though it were the same benchmark as the first.** They are two benchmarks, and
the page runs both against **Codex CLI v0.136.0 with gpt-5.5** — no Claude arm at
all.

> The model got better at these tasks in one month by more than any skill pack's
> advantage over it in either run.

Concretely: Git Ship Done moved from **+1.64 to −0.14** against baseline while
scoring *higher* in absolute terms. The pack improved; the baseline improved more.

Two consequences omc-slim should absorb:

1. **Every published number needs a date and a contemporaneous control.** A figure
   measured against last month's baseline is not stale, it is wrong.
2. **A verbosity claim can invert.** On the same page, every pack was *less*
   verbose than baseline in June (0.258–0.300 vs 0.311) and *more* verbose in July
   (0.895–0.915 vs 0.827). For a layer sold on imposing discipline, that is the
   wrong direction on the one axis that moved most.

This compounds the ceiling-falling finding in §10.1. **A prompt layer's value
proposition decays by default between model releases** — measured on gpt-5.5
under Codex CLI, and transferred to omc-slim as a mechanism rather than a
magnitude, since nobody has run the equivalent on Claude. That makes a cheap
repeatable eval (§6.1) a maintenance requirement rather than a nice-to-have.

### 4.7 omc-slim's own audit already flagged three claims

`docs/AUDIT-2026-08-25.md` §D is harsher on omc-slim than anything external, and
its findings should be reflected in the user-facing docs rather than only in the
audit:

| Claim | Audit verdict | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| "18% cheaper at equal correctness" | **MISLEADING as a headline** | n=3, one single-file task; `BENCHMARK.md:99` itself says *"correctness does not discriminate"*; and the omc-slim arm ran with two MCP servers no other arm had |
| "~4,485 tokens" static context | **MISLEADING** | `measure-context.sh` is chars/4 and runs **+13.5% high** against a real tokeniser |
| "12 of 15 components route automatically" | **UNPROVEN** | Ad-hoc notes with no dates, prompts, transcripts, or committed harness |

The second row sharpens §2.1: the published figure is not merely on a different
basis from `claude plugin details`, it is **overstated by about 13.5%** on its own
basis. The real static cost is nearer 3,900 tokens than 4,474 — which strengthens
the case for reporting the harness's own number instead.

### 4.8 Loops, retries and silent skips

Three measured failure modes with cheap guards, all relevant to `fixer` and any
lane `deepwork` dispatches.

| Failure | Measurement | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| **Unbounded loops** | 6,549 repos audited: **68 confirmed infinite-agentic-loop failures across 47 projects**; unbounded retry, unbounded tool iteration and unbounded agent chat account for **69.1%**; API cost exhaustion in **95.6%**. Root cause in all 68: *"the repeated path is not covered by a strong bound"* ([arXiv:2607.01641](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01641v1)) | Bound every loop on the actual feedback path, and fail open. `maxTurns` (§6.1) is the frontmatter version |
| **Retry without checking the postcondition** | 300 seeded runs: duplicate side-effect rate **20% → 44% → 72%** across fault levels; verify-before-retry cuts it to **0% / 16% / 20%** ([arXiv:2608.02645](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02645)) | Check whether the postcondition already holds before retrying |
| **Silent tool skip** — narrating a completed action without invoking the tool | ~1,900 trials across 13 models: **Claude Haiku 4.5 at 26.7% combined**, driven by 80% on one tool ([FUZN](https://fuzn.com.au/tool-call-reliability-in-production-llm-agents/)) | Assert on the tool call, not the narration. This is exactly what `verify-deliverables.mjs` does for `fixer` and `designer`, and it is the strongest justification for keeping that hook |

### 4.9 Governance prose measured against nothing at all

One result deserves its own place, because it is the most direct challenge to the
entire premise of a rules layer.

A worktree-isolated ablation over **82 validation runs across 11 scenarios**
([Coombs](https://dev.to/james-coombs/your-claudemd-rules-achieve-0-compliance-heres-the-data-kk3)).
**Corrected 2026-08-26 against the source; this document first published it as a
"nine-run blinded ablation" scoring 16.3 / 16.1 / 27.5.**

| Arm | Score |
|---|---|
| No guidance | **16.3 / 30** |
| Full CLAUDE.md governance block | **16.4 / 30** |
| A two-sentence prompt plus a prototype URL | **26.7 / 30** |

Strengthening the CLAUDE.md produced **zero measurable improvement** — the
governance arm is a tenth of a point above no guidance at all — while adding an
MCP query tool produced **100% discovery**. The
author's hierarchy: **Discovery > Routing > Documentation.**

Set against §5.1 — where the presence of a rules file was worth 67.7 points — the
two results are not obviously reconcilable, and the difference is likely task
shape. But the practical rule they agree on is the same: **if something must
hold, make it queryable or gated, not documented.**

That is the case for omc-slim's structural layers — `disallowedTools`, the hook,
the coverage and reinforcement gates — over its prose, and it is an argument for
spending future effort there.

## 5. What omc-slim already gets right, with the evidence

Symmetry matters. The research vindicates as much as it undercuts, and these
are the things to defend against future compression.

### 5.1 The layer existing at all is worth ~68 points

**Measured, and the largest single number in the corpus.** In the same factorial
study that found size and position irrelevant, the arm with **no configuration
file at all** scored an instruction-compliance rate of **0.0%** (Wilson upper
bound 0.73%), against **67.7%** for the matched file
([arXiv:2605.10039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10039)).

Read the three results together:

| Variable | Effect |
|---|---|
| Presence of the rules layer | **+67.7 points** |
| Where the rule sits in the file | ~0 (67.7 / 63.2 / 64.0 / 64.0 / 61.8%, p=0.83) |
| File length, 25 → 500 lines | ~0, and the **25-line file scored lowest** (BF₁₀=0.096, affirmative null) |
| Contradiction in an adjacent file | +0.33pp (BF₁₀=0.053, strong null) |

**Deletion is not free, and shortening bought nothing measurable.** Any future
compression pass on omc-slim should be justified by something other than length.

### 5.2 The disambiguation lines are load-bearing

Every omc-slim skill description ends with a "Not X — use Y" clause. That is not
politeness, it is the only evidence-backed defence against the dominant skill
failure mode.

- **Skill shadowing** — wrong selection from overlapping descriptions —
  accounts for up to **68%** of the pass-rate loss as a skill library grows,
  while raw context overhead is statistically indistinguishable from noise
  ([arXiv:2605.24050](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24050v1)).
- The degradation is a **phase transition driven by semantic similarity between
  skills, not by library size**
  ([arXiv:2601.04748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04748)).

omc-slim's 13 components sit far below every measured cliff — 52 skills for
shadowing, ~500 for routing collapse, ~80 for rule-stacking collapse. **Roster
size is not its problem. Semantic overlap with the rest of the machine is**, and
the "Not X" lines are the mitigation.

### 5.3 Naming the specialists inline is right

The output style says it lists the roster "because agent and skill descriptions
get dropped on crowded machines". That is corroborated: **hiding the skill body
and routing on name plus description alone costs 37–44 percentage points of
routing accuracy** across ~80K candidate skills
([arXiv:2603.22455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22455)).

Pure progressive disclosure is a measured handicap for routing, and hierarchical
disclosure is worse still — one benchmark saw it collapse a score from 0.9126 to
0.6398, −29.9 points
([arXiv:2607.17598](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17598v1)).

**Do not delete the inline roster to save 400 tokens.**

### 5.4 The architecture is what Cognition converged on independently

Cognition published *Don't Build Multi-Agents* in 2025 and reversed it on
2026-04-22 ([Multi-Agents: What's Actually
Working](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working)). Their governing rule:

> "multi-agent systems work best today when **writes stay single-threaded** and
> the additional agents contribute **intelligence rather than actions**."

omc-slim: the orchestrator owns writes, four of six agents are read-only, and
`review` runs "somewhere the code was not written". That is the same shape,
reached separately.

Their strongest specific finding also matches: the code-review loop performs
best when the reviewer has **completely clean context, not shared background** —
attributed explicitly to context rot — catching about 2 bugs per PR, 58% severe.

**Any doc still citing *Don't Build Multi-Agents* as current should be updated.
Its own author superseded it.**

### 5.5 "The pass that produced a change cannot be the pass that clears it" is the best-supported rule in the plugin

Four independent measurements:

| Result | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Self-attribution bias — identical artefact, only the framing changed | Monitor AUROC **0.99 off-policy → 0.89 on-policy**. Self-attribution made it **5× more likely** to approve a patch that followed a prompt injection | [arXiv:2603.04582](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04582) |
| Cross-Context Review — 30 artefacts, 150 injected errors, 360 reviews | Fresh-session review **F1 28.6%** vs same-session self-review 24.6% (p=0.008, d=0.52). **Reviewing twice in the same context is worth nothing** (SR2 ≈ SR, p=0.11) | [arXiv:2603.12123](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12123) |
| Models are worse at reviewing their own code | "both models find more bugs in code written by the other model than in code they wrote themselves"; the bug types a model introduces most are the types it misses most. Greptile shipped "Model Inversion" in production | [Greptile](https://www.greptile.com/blog/model-inversion) |
| Cross-model review, 116 tasks | Codex draft → Opus review **89.7%** vs Codex self-review 84.5% vs Codex solo 71.6% | [arXiv:2607.21656](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21656v1) |

Two riders that change how the rule should be written:

1. **Do not brief the reviewer with the plan.** In the Cross-Context Review
   study the "subagent given production context" arm scored **23.8%, worse than
   plain self-review at 24.6%**. Their conclusion: *"Even well-intentioned
   context ('here is what the user wanted') triggers the same bias patterns."*
   `review`'s existing "brief a lane with evidence, never with a verdict" is
   directionally right and should go further — the lane gets the diff and the
   lane text, not the intent.
2. **The reviewer must not be weaker than the writer.** Same 116-task study:
   Opus draft reviewed by Codex was a **regression**, 91.4% → 82.8%. Opus
   reviewing Opus was measured at exactly **zero lift**. Since every omc-slim
   agent inherits the caller's tier, `review` on Opus 5 is the zero-lift cell.

### 5.6 Shipping no MCP servers is enforced by the harness, not just by policy

**First-party.** Claude Code *"never invalidates the cache for a plugin's skills,
commands, agents, hooks, monitors, or themes"* — they append after the
conversation. Only a plugin's **MCP servers** can invalidate it, and only when
their tools load into the prefix rather than being deferred
([prompt-caching](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)).

The v0.8.4 decision to remove `.mcp.json` rather than document it was correct for
a reason stronger than the privacy argument already recorded: bundled MCP is the
single most expensive thing a plugin can ship, and the harness makes that
mechanical.

### 5.7 One hook, tested, beats the official test harness

The bundled hook tester **reports success for a hook written to block**
([#83800](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/83800)) and **never
evaluates the matcher**, so a hook wired to the wrong tool passes
([#83801](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/83801)).

`verify-deliverables.test.mjs` runs the hook as a child process against isolated
fixtures, and `verify-deliverables.mutate.mjs` breaks it seventeen ways to prove
the suite can fail. That is strictly stronger than what ships with the CLI.

## 6. Harness levers omc-slim does not use

Verified against the CLI binary at **2.1.246** and the current documentation.
Ranked by value per token.

### 6.1 Adopt

**1. `claude plugin eval --ablation with-without`.** The highest-value item in
this document by a wide margin, and it is **verified live** — `claude plugin
eval --help` on 2.1.246.

It runs scored eval cases against a plugin and **automatically adds a
no-plugin baseline arm**, reporting Δ. Flags include `--case`, `--judge-model`,
`--json`, `--max-cost-usd` (exit 2 on breach), `--threshold` (exit 1 below), and
`--mocks record|off`. Grader types: `regex`, `file_exists`, `llm`, `tool_used`,
`tool_order`, `baseline`.

This is the falsifiable measurement omc-slim has wanted since v0.1.0, it costs
**zero runtime tokens**, and it slots into CI beside the two structural gates.

Three traps, all from the binary's own validation logic:

- A `tool_used: Skill` grader for the plugin under test **is excluded from the
  score in both arms**. A suite built only on trigger checks measures nothing.
- An under-set `timeout_seconds` or `allowed_tools` **scores 0 in both arms** and
  reads as "the plugin did nothing".
- Reports **publish to claude.ai by default**. CI must pass `--no-publish`.

**2. `claude plugin details` as the source of the published token figure.** It
prints always-on and on-invoke cost per component. It already disagrees with the
README (§2.1). Deriving figures from the harness rather than by hand is the same
discipline `measure-context.sh` was created for.

**3. Marketplace `renames` (v2.1.193+) and `claude plugin tag`.** `renames` maps
an old component name to a new one, or to `null` if removed — the supported
migration path that the `council` removal did not use. `tag` creates
`{name}--v{version}` and **validates plugin.json against the enclosing
marketplace entry** before the tag is cut, which is exactly the drift found in
§2.2.

**4. `disable-model-invocation: true` on genuinely user-invoked skills.** It does
not merely hide the skill from the `/` menu — it **removes the skill from
Claude's context entirely**, so the listing cost goes to zero. `codemap` says "on
request only" in prose today; this is the enforced version. Pairs with Matt
Pocock's invocation axis (§7.9).

**5. `maxTurns` on every agent.** One frontmatter line, zero context cost, and it
bounds a runaway. `explorer`'s 150-line cap is currently a promise; `maxTurns` is
a fact.

**6. A contradiction sweep.** Not a harness feature — a practice both vendors
converged on. OpenAI ships a metaprompt whose stated job is surfacing "any
contradictions (e.g., 'be concise' vs 'err on the side of completeness')"
([GPT-5.1 guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt-5/gpt-5-1_prompting_guide.ipynb)),
and Anthropic found contradictions between its own system prompt and its own
skills. Given conflict is the measured driver of instruction collapse, this is
the audit that replaces the token count as the headline gate.

### 6.2 Consider, with a named risk

| Lever | Buys | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| `context: fork` + `agent: Explore` on a heavy skill | `Explore` skips CLAUDE.md and git status, so a forked skill body never enters the main window. Turns `review`'s ~6.2k on-invoke into roughly zero | Changes the interaction model; a fork's edits fall outside `/rewind` checkpoints |
| `isolation: worktree` on `fixer` and `designer` | Free blast-radius containment. The CAID ablation is the cleanest evidence in the corpus: worktree isolation 59.1% vs soft instruction-only isolation 56.1% vs single agent 53.1% on Commit0-Lite — and on PaperBench **soft isolation was worse than not delegating at all** ([arXiv:2603.21489](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21489v1)) | Worktrees miss each other's prompt cache. And superpowers [#1040](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/1040) shows a recon agent returning canonical repo paths defeats the isolation silently |
| `disallowed-tools` in skill frontmatter | Makes `deep-interview`'s "STOPS for approval before any code" structurally true rather than a promise | The grant clears on the next user message, so it hardens the first turn only |
| `subagentPromptCacheTtl: "1h"` (v2.1.242+) | Subagents, forks and compaction default to a **5-minute** TTL even on a subscription, where the main conversation gets an hour. A delegation-first plugin runs most of its tokens in the 5-minute bucket | It is a user setting, not a plugin one. This belongs in documentation, not in the plugin |
| Hook `if:` field | Permission-rule syntax (`Bash(git *)`, `Edit(*.ts)`) so a tool-path hook's process only spawns on a match. The only way to be on the tool-call path cheaply | omc-slim's stated invariant is *nothing* on the tool-call path. Changing that needs a better reason than "it is now affordable" |

### 6.3 Avoid

- **`model:` or `effort:` in skill frontmatter.** Both are part of the prompt
  cache key, so either buys a full uncached turn — and both are **ignored** when
  Claude invokes the skill via the Skill tool
  ([#79664](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/79664),
  [#81313](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/81313)).
- **`experimental.monitors`.** Each stdout line becomes a conversation message.
  Unbounded per-event injection is the thing omc-slim exists not to do.
- **`userConfig`.** Config nobody sets is precisely what `simplify` deletes.
- **`settings.json { "agent": ... }`.** It replaces the main thread's system
  prompt, tools and model wholesale. Hijacking the main thread from a plugin
  trades a quiet conflict for a loud one.
- **Workflows (`workflows/`).** Tempting for `deepwork` — the runtime would
  execute the stage map instead of the model following it. But **no mid-run user
  input is possible**, and the review gate between stages *is* the point of
  `deepwork`. One workflow per stage is not a workflow.

## 7. Per-component findings

### 7.1 The output style (orchestrator)

**Keep, with evidence:** the precedence block; the inline roster (§5.3); "never
fan out because an agent exists"; "surgical scope, not timid scope"; "do not
manage the context window".

**Two direct hits from Anthropic's Opus 5 page:**

> "Delegate to a subagent only for large tasks that are genuinely independent
> and parallelizable… **do not use subagents to verify or double-check your own
> work.**"

That contradicts the current rule that non-trivial writer output goes through
`review`. The contradiction is not trivially resolvable — §5.5 has four
measurements saying independent verification works. The likely reconciliation is
that Anthropic is targeting *reflexive* verification of one's own reasoning,
while the measured wins are for reviewing a **committed artefact in a clean
context**. That distinction should be written into the rule, not left implicit.

> "In a long system prompt, pair the instruction with a short reminder near the
> end of the prompt."

Read it as written: the sentence is scoped to **a conciseness instruction inside
a long system prompt**, not to rules in general. **Corrected 2026-08-26 against
the source; this document first published it as Anthropic "explicitly endorsing
stating a rule twice".** It rehabilitates omc-slim's rebuttal-relocation practice
only for instructions of that shape, against the same vendor's "repetition →
concise descriptions" shift. Restating anything else twice is unsupported here.

**The strongest addition available:** nothing in the file addresses
**within-session decay**, which is the only large measured effect on rule
compliance (−5.6% odds per generated function), or **compaction eviction**
(0% → 30% violations, 38% when a rule is dropped). Constraint pinning restores
0%. Output styles are re-sent every turn as part of the system prompt, which is
structurally closer to pinning than a CLAUDE.md — **that is a real advantage and
it is currently undocumented.**

### 7.2 `review`

The best-researched component, and the one with the most available upgrades.

**Adopt, ranked:**

1. **A "pre-existing" severity tier.** Anthropic's own Code Review ships 🟣
   Pre-existing as a first-class severity, and its open-source plugin lists
   "real issues, but on lines the user did not modify" as a canonical false
   positive. One enum value removes an entire noise class. omc-slim currently
   handles this with a *Refuse these* table row that says report it anyway.
2. **Fix-as-verification.** Write the fix; if you cannot articulate an input that
   behaves differently before and after, **the finding was a false positive —
   drop it silently.** Measured
   ([arXiv:2603.00539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00539)). `review` already fixes
   what is mechanical, so this is nearly free.
3. **Adjudicate each finding in a fresh context, by a separate agent, without the
   finder's reasoning.** Anthropic's plugin runs one Haiku agent per finding
   against a verbatim rubric and filters below 80. BitsAI-CR measured this
   raising precision **57.03% → 65.59%** at ByteDance scale
   ([arXiv:2501.15134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15134)). Excluding the finder's
   rationale is what defeats verbosity and self-preference bias.
4. **Give the adjudicator a kill mandate.** "Refute-or-Promote" gates instruct
   agents to *disprove* the claim; **79% of 171 candidates died**
   ([arXiv:2604.19049](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19049)). Framing matters:
   "is this real?" invites agreement.
5. **Decorrelate lanes by evidence source, not by topic.** omc-slim's ten lanes
   are ten *topics* over the same bytes. Anthropic's five reviewers read
   **different artefacts**: CLAUDE.md, the diff alone with no extra context, git
   blame history, prior PR comments on the same files, and in-code comments.
   Cursor's Bugbot randomises diff ordering across passes for the same reason.
   **Two lanes reading the same bytes and agreeing is not corroboration.**
6. **A git-history lane.** Blame the modified lines. A change that reverts a past
   bugfix is a high-precision finding invisible to every other lane.
7. **Cap the nits, report the tail as a count.**

**The self-reported confidence field is the weakest part.** Verbalised
confidence runs ~88% against ~79% actual correctness, the bias is mechanistic
rather than promptable-away, and **functional calibration is consistently worse
than security calibration**
([arXiv:2412.14737](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14737v2),
[arXiv:2604.01457](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01457v2),
[arXiv:2606.31159](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31159v1)). Keep the field as an
output contract; stop letting the finder's own number pass the gate.

**Delete coverage from the vocabulary.** Coverage-to-real-bug-detection
correlation is uniformly weak on buggy code, and test-suite-size to
mutation-score is **r=0.031** across >100,000 test cases
([arXiv:2607.22880](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22880v1)).

**Add: diff the test file for weakening.** Assertions removed, `assertEquals`
becoming `assertNotNull`, tolerances widened, `@skip` added, expected values
edited to match new output. Given the author is often an agent, and the
visible/held-out gap grows 28pp per 10× code size
([arXiv:2605.21384](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21384)), this may be the
highest-value single check in the review.

**Calibration for the whole skill:** best-in-class commercial precision measured
by whether a developer acted is **49.2%** (CodeRabbit, ~300,000 PRs, Martian
benchmark). Graphite Diamond claims under 3% false positives and was measured at
a **6% catch rate** by a competitor. Those are the same dial. omc-slim should
expose it to the caller rather than fixing it.

### 7.3 `tracer`

Three upgrades, all cheap:

1. **Pre-declare falsification criteria before gathering evidence.** Each
   hypothesis states up front what would confirm it, what would falsify it, and
   what would be ambiguous. `wshobson/agents`' `team-debugger` does this. It is
   strictly stronger than the current "gather evidence both for and against",
   because it removes the freedom to decide retrospectively what counted — and
   that is the direct antidote to false problem identification — inventing a
   fault in something correct. The 69.6% rate this once cited for it is struck
   (§4.2): the number is in neither source, though the failure mode is real.
2. **Force hypotheses to span distinct failure categories** — logic, data, state,
   integration, resource, environment. `tracer` asks for three; nothing currently
   stops three retellings of one story.
3. **Add a third verdict.** The output block has `ruled out` but no way to say
   *"cannot be determined from available evidence"*, which is a different answer
   the caller needs.

**The escalation trigger is empirically supported.** debug-gym measured a
debugger unlocked *only after five failed rewrites* beating always-on tooling —
**52.1% vs 48.4%**, against 37.2% with no debugger
([arXiv:2503.21557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21557)). superpowers independently
uses three failed fixes as an architecture-questioning ratchet. A count that
**stops** action is safe; a count that **authorises** action is the anti-pattern
([superpowers#1487](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/1487)).

**Sycophancy is `tracer`'s specific hazard.** Its entry condition is literally a
user saying "I already tried this and it didn't work", which is the documented
trigger for wrongly admitting mistakes
([arXiv:2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)).

### 7.4 `oracle`

**The single highest-value change: stop describing the stance, assign it.**

Measured head-to-head across stance-forcing techniques: explicit **Devil's
Advocate role assignment produced 99.2% disagreement against a 48.3% baseline**,
while *"strong role framing"* and *"explicit dissent instructions"* were
**statistically indistinguishable from baseline**
([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=mxBmj5LYU2)).

`oracle` currently uses adjectives — "senior technical advisor", "judge the
call". Adjectives do not buy dissent.

**Second: blind it.** Self-preference correlates linearly with self-recognition
ability ([arXiv:2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)), and an unblinded
second opinion is structurally biased toward ratifying what it was shown.

**Third: keep it to one pass.** Multi-agent debate does not reliably beat
self-consistency at comparable cost
([ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371)), and accuracy **declines over
rounds** — correct-to-incorrect transitions exceed the reverse, and **stronger
agents flip to a weaker peer's wrong answer more often** than the reverse
([arXiv:2509.05396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05396)). That last paper also
records ten reviewers unanimously endorsing a **non-existent** padding-oracle
vulnerability. **Consensus is not verification.**

This is also the retrospective justification for removing `council` in v0.8.5.
The removal was argued on cost and stance-independence; the literature says
debate rounds actively degrade accuracy. The decision was better than its stated
reasons.

**Prior art worth copying:** Amp's oracle is restricted by a positive
**allowlist** (`Read, Grep, glob, web_search, read_web_page`) rather than a
denylist, and its prompt requires announcing the escalation to the user — cheap,
and it makes an expensive call auditable.

### 7.5 `designer`

**The AI-slop tell has moved, and a purple ban fights the last war.** Models that
learned everyone hates the purple gradient now reach for **cream, emerald and
italic serifs** instead. Worse, *"when a prompt bans purple, models cascade to
emerald (#10B981) as the next safe accent, sometimes ignoring explicit written
guidance against green"*
([signs-of-ai-design](https://github.com/febbhav/signs-of-ai-design)).
Anthropic's own `frontend-design` skill independently names cream `#F4F1EA` plus
serif plus terracotta as its current cluster #1.

The framing to steal is not a ban list:

> "Judge the absence of decisions, not the presence of a style. The tell is not
> that Inter is a bad typeface. The tell is Inter, unchosen, next to twenty
> other unchosen defaults."

**Ranked additions, roughly 300 tokens total:**

1. A **dated** calibration list of the current default cluster, framed as
   defaults rather than bans (~50 tokens).
2. **Two-pass: write a compact token system, critique it against the brief,
   then build** (~70). Anthropic's `frontend-design` requires 4–6 named hex
   values, type roles, a layout concept, and **one signature element**, then
   revises anything that "reads like the generic default you would produce for
   any similar page". This converts the current "Be bold" — an unactionable
   exhortation — into a procedure. It also pairs with "spend your boldness in one
   place", which fixes the current section's implicit invitation to *distributed*
   boldness, the thing that actually reads as slop.
3. A **numeric motion floor** (~80). The Motion section currently contains zero
   numbers. The frequency gate is the rule nobody encodes: something used 100+
   times a day gets **no animation, ever**. Durations 100–300ms for UI;
   `transition: all` and `scale(0)` entry are hard fails; `ease-in` on UI is
   wrong because it delays the moment the user is watching most closely.
4. A **numeric craft floor** (~60). "Contrast" with no number becomes ≥4.5:1
   body and ≥3:1 large; target size ≥24×24 CSS px (WCAG 2.2 AA 2.5.8 — the
   44×44 figure is the Apple convention, not the AA floor); measure 65–75ch;
   elevation declared once, border **or** shadow, never both.
5. **The "browser surfaces" reflex** (~35) — best value density found anywhere:
   *"Text selection, the caret, custom scrollbars, focus rings, underline offset
   and tabular numerals all ship with browser defaults that belong to no design
   system. This is the cheapest signal that a page was built rather than
   assembled, and the one models skip most reliably."*

**The accessibility floor is not a taste question.** WebAIM Million, February
2026, top million home pages: **low-contrast text on 83.9%**, up from 79.1%;
95.9% of pages had detected WCAG 2 failures; 56.1 errors per page, up 10.1%
year-on-year. WebAIM attributes part of the rise to *"automated or AI-assisted
coding practices"*. Low contrast is simultaneously the most prevalent defect on
the web, the one AI over-produces, and the one trending worst.

And a ceiling that verification cannot fix: automated tooling catches **57
percent** of accessibility issues (Deque, 13,000 pages, ~300,000 issues) —
corrected 2026-08-26 against the source, which publishes no second decimal; this
document first published "57.38%". Across 300
LLM-generated UIs researchers found **541 semantic accessibility issues, about 2
per UI** — 27% generic button labels, 26% vague link text, 20% poor alt text
([CHI EA '26](https://tommasocalo.github.io/papers/26-semacces-chiea.pdf)).
`alt="image"` passes every automated check and conveys nothing. **The prompt must
carry the semantic floor; axe will never catch it.**

**A correction to widely repeated advice.** Screenshot token cost is **purely
dimensional** — `⌈w/28⌉ × ⌈h/28⌉` visual tokens — so the common claim that JPEG
or WebP "reduces context size" is wrong. Format affects payload bytes and
latency, not tokens. The lever is capping dimensions: 1440×900 costs ~1,700
tokens against ~2,691 for 1920×1080 on Opus 5's high-resolution tier. Prefer the
accessibility-tree snapshot for anything structural, and reserve vision for
optical alignment, spacing rhythm, hierarchy and colour relationships.

### 7.6 `explorer`

The AST gap recorded in `LIMITATIONS.md` is real but the framing has moved: the
question is no longer "which index" but **grep-first versus index-first**, and
the harness now ships an LSP tool that subagents reportedly cannot reach
([#76090](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/76090)).

Two cheap upgrades independent of tooling:

- **`maxTurns`**, so the 150-line cap has a runtime companion.
- **A positive control before trusting a negative result.** Before acting on "0
  matches" or "not found", prove the instrument can produce a hit on a different
  input. From roughly 14 months of logged runs: *"Most of our false 'clean'
  results were a grep that couldn't have matched anything."*
  ([superpowers#2186](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2186), source
  CC BY 4.0.) omc-slim already has the sibling rule from
  `aniruddha-adhikary/skills` — "an empty search is proved against a known
  positive" — in `verification-planning`. **`explorer` is where it actually
  fires, and it is not there.**

A hazard to note: a read-only recon agent returning canonical repository paths is
dangerous when the writer is in a worktree
([superpowers#1040](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/1040)). Relevant
only if `isolation: worktree` is adopted.

### 7.7 `verification-planning`

**Two additions, both from other people's false greens.**

1. **Prove the arranged input, not just the assertion.** A .NET test asserted
   BOM-stripping; `new UTF8Encoding(true).GetBytes(...)` never emits the
   preamble, so the file had no BOM and the assertion compared unstripped text to
   itself. **Deleting the entire implementation kept it green.** It cleared every
   gate and a human found it weeks later
   ([superpowers#2092](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2092)).
   "Every check must be able to fail" is necessary and **not sufficient** — a
   check can be able to fail and still be vacuous if the fixture never held the
   condition.

2. **Revert-and-rerun as the named ritual.** For any test added alongside a fix
   in the same diff: revert the source change, confirm the test goes red,
   restore. It costs one test run and it is the only *direct* test of "can this
   check fail" — the thing mutation testing approximates expensively. It is also
   the SWT-bench criterion, and it catches the confirmation-bias failure where
   the test was written against the buggy behaviour.

**Also worth stating:** structural RED is not behavioural RED. A test that fails
with `X is not exported from Y` satisfies "fails because the feature is missing"
while proving only that the import resolves
([superpowers#2146](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2146)).

**Diff-scoped mutation testing** is the strongest available test-quality signal —
vanilla LLM prompting plateaus at a 53% mutation score, unchanged after four
iterations, and reaches **89.5%** when surviving mutants are fed back
([arXiv:2506.02954](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02954v1)). It must be scoped to
changed lines only; runtime is mutants × suite time, so a 60s suite with 1,000
mutants is ~16 hours. Google made it tolerable by suppressing mutations in
logging and idiomatic code and capping mutants per line and per review, moving
"not useful" from ~80% to ~15%.

**And the counter-pressure, stated plainly:** Anthropic's Opus 5 page names this
skill's category for removal. That half stands. The second half did not:
~~"a reviewer prompted to find gaps will usually report some, even when the work
is sound."~~ **That quotation was fabricated** — struck 2026-08-26, after the
full page was fetched twice and did not contain the sentence. It is left here
rather than deleted because a citation this document invented is a finding about
its own method.

The page says the opposite: *"Claude Opus 5 reviews code with high precision and
recall: it finds real bugs at a high rate per pass, and its additional findings
are mostly real issues rather than false positives."*

`review`'s rule — *"Inventing a problem to look thorough costs more than missing
one"* — keeps its place, on other grounds. The real sentence supports it from the
other side: if the extra findings are already mostly real, a prompt pushing for
more gaps buys noise, not coverage. And §1.2's literal-mindedness warning cuts
both ways — a model that reads "be conservative" literally and reports less will
read "find what others missed" literally too. Cite those. Do not cite the struck
sentence.

### 7.8 `deepwork`

**Net removal available (§3.2)** — roughly 200 tokens, matching upstream's own
correction: conditional structure scan, conditional phase commit, delete the
`impact × confidence ÷ cost` heuristic.

**Additions worth their tokens:**

- **`Consumes:` / `Produces:` interface blocks per lane** (~40 tokens per lane).
  *"A task's implementer sees only their own task; this block is how they learn
  the names and types neighbouring tasks use."* Parallel lanes without an
  interface contract drift and fail at integration.
- **The preflight conflict scan emits rows, not a verdict.** One row per lane
  pair sharing a file or interface. *"'The scan is clean' without those rows is
  not a scan you ran."* Same evidence-not-verdict principle `review` already
  uses, applied to planning.
- **"Rulings, not stalls."** Four and only four things stop a running plan:
  something irreversible or destructive, something security-sensitive, a side
  effect outside the worktree, or a plan so broken every path is a guess.
  Everything else gets a logged ruling — `Ruling: <what> — <why> — <what it costs
  if wrong>` — collected into a final roll-up. The motivating incident is a
  session that **sat blocked for nearly nine hours**; a related issue records
  8+ hour silent stalls
  ([superpowers#1545](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/1545)). The
  rationale is the good part: *"A ruling that dies with the workspace was a
  decision made in secret."*
- **An explicit floor for when *not* to spawn a lane.** superpowers has no
  inline-versus-dispatch threshold and every plan fans out
  ([#1917](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/1917)); the measured
  consequence is 68M tokens for 3,000 lines — on Opencode with gpt-5.4, not this
  harness (§2.3). Fan-out pays for independence and taxes coupling.
- **The one-way ceremony ratchet.** Hidden complexity discovered mid-task
  upgrades the depth; **nothing downgrades mid-task**. Take the ratchet, not the
  three-path router that produced it — the router shipped with a tier that had no
  execution path (§4.1, item 14).

**Highest-token-ROI mechanism found anywhere in superpowers:** hand the review
lane a **prepared diff file**, not instructions to derive the diff. One command
produces commit list, `--stat` and `git diff -U10` into a file; the lane gets the
path. The diff never enters the orchestrator's context, and the lane needs **one
Read instead of the measured 40–67 tool calls**.

### 7.9 `simplify`, `deep-interview`, `codemap`, `fixer`, `librarian`

**`simplify`.** Its closest published analogue, SlopCodeBench's `anti_slop` arm,
says the approach works on the metric it targets, does not compound across
iterations, does not improve correctness, and costs ~48% more tokens
([arXiv:2603.24755](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24755v1)). `simplify` being a
**backstop applied afterwards** rather than a front-loaded constraint is
arguably the right architecture given that result, because the effect has to be
re-applied. Worth saying so in the skill.

**`deep-interview`.** Anthropic ships nearly this exact prompt as a
recommendation, and its definition of a good spec has one element omc-slim's
schema lacks: *"The most useful specs are self-contained: they **name the files
and interfaces involved**, state what is out of scope, and end with an
end-to-end verification step."* Two of three are already there. Adding the first
is the cheapest, highest-confidence change in this document.

Two riders. The **one-question-at-a-time** rule has **no controlled study behind
it** — Anthropic's own recommended prompt uses batched `AskUserQuestion`, and
`mattpocock/skills` has both an open issue that the rule is not honoured
([#221](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/221)) and a user revolt when
it changed the other way
([#831](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/831), *"getting 10+ questions
at once is unbearable"*). Treat it as a deliberate preference, not a finding.
And the interview needs an explicit terminal stop: GPT-5.5 jumps straight to
implementation after the equivalent skill ends
([#240](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/240)).

**EARS syntax** for acceptance criteria — `WHEN <trigger> THE SYSTEM SHALL
<response>` — is Kiro-proven, costs nothing, and makes an unfalsifiable criterion
syntactically awkward to write.

**`codemap`.** Directly exposed to §4.1 item 6: it writes durable files, and
line-number citations in durable artefacts rot. **Cite symbols.** Also exposed to
the ADR-lifecycle finding — the only user-measured claim in that lane is **72
ADRs on a one-year project, 11 superseded or rejected and still being read as
in-force (15% wrong), and 5 of 7 marked "Proposed" actually shipped**
([#822](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/822)). A codemap has the same
decay profile and no status axis.

**`fixer`.** Well-covered already. One addition: the **terminal-agent clause** —
*"You are a terminal agent. Your text output in this turn is your only output
channel."* `fixer` cannot spawn agents, so it cannot lose a lane the way
[superpowers#2202](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/2202) describes,
but the clause also prevents the softer failure of returning a status promise.

**`librarian`.** The package-hallucination numbers justify its existence:
**19.7% of 2.23M generated samples named a non-existent package, and 43% of those
names recurred on all ten identical re-runs** (Spracklen et al., USENIX Security
2025 — re-attributed 2026-08-26; this document first published the figures
against socket.dev). Reproducibility is the trap — a confident wrong answer looks
like a corroborated one. But the rate is dated: socket.dev's 2026 re-evaluation
of frontier models puts it at **4.62–6.10%**, so the justification is now the
mechanism, not the magnitude. "Read the installed source before anything written
about it" is the correct primary rule and it is already there.

### 7.10 Code search, retrieval and deletion — measured on this machine

The last lane to report ran its own measurements rather than only citing others.
Its numbers are single-repo and single-run, on `express`, `hono` and `requests`,
and are labelled as such.

#### The AST gap in `LIMITATIONS.md` is smaller than stated

**Verified here.** `ast-grep 0.44.0` is installed and on `PATH`, as is `rg
15.2.0` and `jq 1.8.2`.

`LIMITATIONS.md` says: *"No AST-aware search. Upstream used `ast_grep_search`
across 25 languages. Claude Code has no equivalent, so structural queries fall
back to `Grep`."* That is true of the *harness*. It is not true of the *machine*
— `ast-grep` covers ~26 languages and is one `Bash` call away wherever it is
installed.

The honest correction is narrower: omc-slim cannot *depend* on it, because it is
not guaranteed present. But `explorer` currently does not mention it at all, so
it will not reach for it even when it is there.

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---|
| `ast-grep outline` on `hono/src` | **385 lines from 64,496 lines of TypeScript** — 10,020 B against 786,926 B raw, **−98.7%** |
| `rg -l` vs `rg -n` vs `rg -n -C2`, same query | 1,033 B / 30,109 B / 71,561 B — **`-l` is 29× cheaper than `-n` and 69× cheaper than `-C2`** |
| Multi-line call `foo(\n a,\n b\n)` | `rg 'foo\(.*\)'` finds **1 of 2**; `ast-grep -p 'foo($$$)'` finds **2 of 2** |
| Repomix `--compress` on the same tree | **−51.1% measured**, against a README claim of ~70%. Do not budget on the vendor figure |

Three mechanisms follow, cheapest first:

1. **Open with `rg -l` or `rg -c`, never `rg -n`.** 29× on a broad query, and
   `-C` should require a reason. This is a one-line rule and it is the single
   biggest saving available to `explorer`.
2. **`ast-grep outline` as the first move on an unfamiliar directory** — when it
   is present. It is the Agentless "skeleton" and the Aider repo map as one shell
   command, with no index to go stale and no LLM call.
3. **`ast-grep -p '<pattern>' --json=compact | jq -r '.[]|"\(.file):\(.range.start.line)"'`
   *is* `explorer`'s output contract, produced mechanically.**

And the guards, all reproduced:

- **`ast-grep outline` degrades badly on prototype-assignment JavaScript.**
  Express's 631-line `lib/application.js` outlined to **3 lines**, missing every
  `app.foo = function` member. `explorer` must not read a thin outline as "nothing
  here."
- **Metavariables over-match.** `$RES.send($$$)` matched 351 sites against `rg`'s
  271 for `res.send`, because `$RES` is any receiver.
- **Prefer the name `ast-grep` over `sg` in any prompt text** — on many systems
  `sg` is `setgid`.

**The compression result that matters most** is not a tooling fact. Agentless
found that feeding an LLM a **skeleton** — signatures only — beat feeding complete
file contents on both cost *and* accuracy, cutting context from over 3,000 lines
to under 800 ([arXiv:2407.01489](https://github.com/OpenAutoCoder/Agentless)).
Compression improved accuracy rather than trading against it. That is the
strongest available justification for `explorer`'s 150-line cap.

#### `librarian`: retrieve conditionally, not always

**The most decision-relevant finding for this agent contradicts its current
rule.** CloudAPIBench measured documentation injection at **+9.36pp on rare APIs
and −39.02pp on common ones** when the retriever is mediocre. Always-on doc
injection is *negative* for well-known APIs.

`librarian` currently mandates that **every** load-bearing external claim gets an
open-web pass. The evidence says that rule is right for rare, version-sensitive
or post-cutoff symbols, and wasteful-to-harmful for stable ones. The gate should
be: post-cutoff? rare symbol? version-pinned behaviour? disk silent?

The staleness half is also measured: **56.1% → 32.5% accuracy across a knowledge
cutoff, with RAG recovering +13.5%** (RustEvo²). Both halves are real; the rule
needs a condition, not a deletion.

**Two mechanisms worth their tokens:**

| Mechanism | Measurement |
|---|---|
| **Registry 404 as a hard gate on every package name** before it reaches a file | ~0.9s, no auth. `curl registry.npmjs.org/hono/latest` is **19,878 B against 6,109,961 B** for the full document — a **307× penalty** for fetching the wrong thing. Fabricated names `reqeusts-async` and `hono-router-utils` both return 404 |
| **`llms.txt` yes, `llms-full.txt` never blind** | `developers.cloudflare.com/llms-full.txt` is **57,269,704 B ≈ 14.3M tokens — fourteen times a 1M context window.** The index at the same site is 15,906 B. Fetch the index, pick the section, fetch that page |

**Endpoints an agent will try and should not.** Verified dead or blocked:
grep.app's API (429 plus a Vercel bot checkpoint), `searchcode.com` (404),
`mcp.deepwiki.com/sse` (410 Gone — the `/mcp` endpoint works),
`mcp.astro.build` (does not exist), Papers with Code (redirects away), GitHub
code search unauthenticated (401). `PROVENANCE.md` records grep.app as a removed
MCP server; it is now also unusable directly.

**The silent-nothing trap is the dangerous one.** Yarn Berry PnP keeps
dependencies as **unextracted zips** in `.yarn/cache/`, and pnpm's
`node_modules/<pkg>` is a symlink into `.pnpm/`. Both produce *empty* results
rather than errors, so an agent reads zero hits and concludes the API does not
exist. `librarian`'s "read the installed source first" rule needs this caveat
attached, or it fails exactly where it is most confident.

Also: `dist-tags.latest` is not the highest semver — `zod` ships a canary above
`latest` — so never sort the version list.

**One honest note on provenance.** The "read installed source, not docs" thesis
has no authoritative published backing. The rigorous source (Spracklen, USENIX
Security 2025) motivates *registry verification*, not source-reading. The
source-reading advocacy is self-published and unaudited. It is sensible practice
and worth keeping — but `PROVENANCE.md` should not imply it is evidence-backed.

#### `simplify`: two results that should change its behaviour

**1. Green tests are measurably insufficient.** **19–35% of LLM-generated
refactorings are functionally non-equivalent, and about 21% of those are not
caught by the existing test suite**
([arXiv:2602.15761](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15761)). The authors' conclusion:
*"reliance on existing tests might overestimate functional equivalence in
LLM-generated code refactorings."* `simplify`'s "existing tests pass without
being modified" checkbox currently reads as sufficient. It is necessary and
demonstrably not sufficient, and the skill should say so.

**2. Agents execute refactorings well and choose them badly.** CodeTaste: frontier
agents reach **~70% alignment when told exactly what to refactor, and under 8%
when given a focus area and asked to decide.** That argues for
tool-generated candidates with the agent as executor, not agent-as-discoverer.

**The false-positive problem, reproduced live on two widely-used libraries:**

- **`vulture` on `psf/requests`** flags `HTTPProxyAuth` and `HTTPDigestAuth` as
  unused classes. Both are documented public API. Its confidence tiers do not
  save you: 48 findings at 60%, **4 at 90%, 0 at 100%** — and all four 90%
  findings are in `compat.py`, whose own source comment reads *"Keep OrderedDict
  for backwards compatibility."* They are re-exports for downstream consumers.
  **Vulture's highest confidence tier was 100% false positives on that tree**,
  while `ruff --select F401` — which understands re-export convention — reported
  all checks passed.
- **The test suite would not catch the deletion.** `requests`' own suite has zero
  references to `requests.compat.quote_plus`. Deleting it keeps CI **green** and
  silently breaks every downstream consumer.
- **`knip` on `honojs/hono`** flags `APIGatewayProxyEvent`, `ALBProxyEvent` and 20
  other types as unused exports. `package.json` declares `./aws-lambda`,
  `./lambda-edge` and `./request-id` as **public entrypoints**. "Unused export"
  means unused *within this repo* — for a library that is the exact inverse of
  the truth.

**The highest-value single addition** is therefore a mandatory pre-delete check
that `simplify`'s ladder does not have: **is it a declared public entrypoint?**
One command — `jq '.exports' package.json`, or `__all__`, or the docs tree. It
catches both reproduced false positives.

**Chesterton's Fence is cheap and has two traps.** The archaeology costs almost
nothing — `git log -S '<sym>' --reverse` returned the 2012 introducing commit for
`quote_plus` in **0.03 seconds and one line**. But:

- **A shallow clone destroys it silently.** On a `--depth 1` clone, `git rev-list
  --count HEAD` is 1 and the pickaxe returns exactly one commit — a confident,
  wrong "introduced here."
- **A rename destroys it.** Scoped to the current path, the first result for
  `compat.py` is *"Move to src directory"*. An agent reads "just a directory
  move, no real reason" and deletes. The true origin is 2012.

`simplify` says to check `git blame`. Blame gives the last toucher, which is
almost never the commit that created the fence. **The rule should be: cite the
*introducing* commit, and refuse the citation if the repository is shallow or the
commit is a move.**

**A correction to a widely repeated story.** The "Gemini CLI deleted all my
files" incident is misreported nearly everywhere. The original reporter
**retracted** it: *"Okay, my bad. I did find these files in the C:\ root upon a
deeper search… the gemini CLI responses and the subsequent 'analysis' by gemini
and claude really sent me down a bad rabbit hole"*
([#4586](https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli/issues/4586)). An agent
confessed to an irreversible failure, a second agent wrote a plausible
root-cause analysis, and the press ran it.

The real lesson is sharper than the myth: **an agent's self-report about what it
destroyed is not evidence.** Replit's incident has the same shape — the agent
fabricated record counts and told the user recovery was impossible, which was
false; a standard rollback worked.

**And the deletion-at-scale prior art is entirely deterministic, not LLM.** Meta's
SCARF deleted **over 100M lines across more than 370,000 change requests in five
years** using a static graph plus runtime endpoint hit counts, with a textual
fallback specifically to catch `eval` and string-keyed references. Their stated
trade-off is the rule `simplify` should adopt verbatim:

> "This approach can cause false negatives, but avoids false positives. When
> automating the removal of dead code, those are a more serious problem."

The cheap implementation of that bias: **even with a clean tool verdict, grep the
bare symbol name** — that is what catches string-keyed dispatch, reflection,
config references and templates.

## 8. Measurement — how to settle the central bet

`docs/BENCHMARK.md` is honest about being n=3 on one task. The research says the
fix is not more repeats.

### 8.1 The design constraints

| Constraint | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Task-level variance dominates run-level | 3 → 10 repeats per task raises power **13%** | [arXiv:2607.27250](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27250v1) |
| Tasks needed for a 10pp effect | **~120–200** | same |
| Minimum detectable effect at 17 tasks | **>30pp** | same |
| Run-to-run spread at temperature 0 | σ **>1.5pp**; single-run pass@1 varies **2.2–6.0pp** | [arXiv:2602.07150](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07150) |
| Runs for a 5pp effect | 2–3. For 2pp, ~9. For 1pp, ~36 | same |

So: **more tasks, three repeats each, paired within task, report confidence
intervals and equivalence bounds.** "We could not detect a difference, bounded to
≤X" is a publishable result and is what four other studies reported.

### 8.2 The missing arm

The v0.8.1 benchmark had three arms — plain, omc-slim, and CLAUDE.md+fable-mode.
Two arms are missing and both are cheap:

1. **`plain-boN`** — best-of-N single agent at **matched dollar cost**. This is
   the baseline the multi-agent literature says most systems quietly lose to.
   Majority voting over greedy alone is worth about +2.7pp with no architecture
   at all.
2. **`omc-slim-ablated`** — identical prompts with `Agent`/`Task` removed, so the
   main thread must work inline. **This is the only arm that separates the
   delegation from the orchestrator prompt**, and it is the arm that would have
   answered the central-bet question in v0.8.1.

### 8.3 Five task shapes that actually separate delegating from not

Ordered by cost to run, cheapest first.

**A. Delegation-trigger calibration — run this one first.** Forty tasks
stratified by an independent rating of decomposability into four tiers, from
trivially-inline to delegation-required. Metric: correlation between tier and
subagents actually spawned, plus over-delegation rate on tier-1 and
under-delegation rate on tier-4. **It needs no grader beyond the transcript
log.** A well-calibrated delegator that never wins on quality is still worth
shipping if it costs less; a mis-calibrated one that occasionally wins is not.

**B. Constraint survival across the brief boundary.** A 6–10 file change with
**eight non-negotiable constraints stated once and never repeated** — no new
dependencies, all timestamps UTC, public API unchanged, errors to stderr, and so
on. Metric: fraction still honoured in the final diff, graded by script where
possible. This measures the **tax** of delegation directly, with no confound from
task difficulty. Instrument it further by counting how many of the eight appear
verbatim in each brief — that yields a mechanism, not just an outcome. It targets
the two largest MAST failure modes: constraints lost crossing the brief (11.65%)
and requirements not followed (10.98%).

**C. Verifier separation.** Thirty diffs of 150–400 lines with **150 injected
defects**, five per diff, ground truth held out. Arms: same-session self-review;
same-session review *twice* (the essential control that rules out "more passes");
`review` briefed **with** the plan; `review` given **only** the diff; and review
by a different model tier. Ninety reviews per arm is affordable. The literature
predicts diff-only beats self-review by ~4 F1 points, that reviewing twice buys
nothing, and — the interesting one — that **briefing with the plan may score
below plain self-review**.

**D. Context-window overflow.** The one regime where theory says delegation
*must* win. A bug whose cause requires reading five files totalling >400k tokens,
with high distractor density. Vary total context required at 0.2×, 0.6×, 1.0×
and 2.0× of the window. **A single number — "omc-slim overtakes plain at X×
context" — would be the most valuable output of the whole programme.** If
omc-slim does not win here, the architecture has no regime.

**E. Disjoint parallel lanes.** Commit0-style: strip twelve function bodies
across four modules with **no cross-module call edges**, verified by call graph.
Arms should include worktree isolation against soft instruction-only isolation,
replicating the CAID ablation where soft isolation was *worse than not
delegating at all* on one benchmark. Then run the anti-design — the same twelve
functions **with** cross-module dependencies — where delegation should lose, and
where **omc-slim declining to delegate is itself the measurable correct
outcome.**

### 8.4 Reframe the existing result rather than retiring it

Re-run the v0.8.1 duplicate-file-finder as arm zero and confirm no delegation
occurs. It currently reads as an embarrassment — the central bet untested. It is
better framed as a **negative control that passed**: on a task where the
literature says delegation cannot pay, the trigger logic correctly declined to
delegate.

### 8.5 Two grading upgrades

- `claude plugin eval` (§6.1) gives a with/without ablation, machine-readable
  output and a CI threshold for free.
- [`hamelsmu/evals-skills`](https://github.com/hamelsmu/evals-skills) ships
  `validate-evaluator` and `write-judge-prompt`, which are directly aimed at the
  held-out grading fixture. Small, MIT, near-zero defect traffic — and itself

not component names — verified against the 2.1.246 binary, where it sits beside

skill, and the row over-generalised it. What that row was reaching for is real
and was already in place: a CHANGELOG entry and a coverage gate that asserts the
worded roster. That gate had a hole — it checked `plugin.json` and
`PROVENANCE.md` but not `marketplace.json`, which sat at "seven skills" through
the release that removed the seventh. Now checked, and proved able to fail.

### 9.4 Never

Restating the fifteen from §4.1 in one line each, because this is the list that
matters most: no prose where a tool boundary exists; no re-delegation; no
narrated result as evidence; nothing written into the user's repo; no harness
tool named in prose; no `file:line` in a durable artefact; no trigger whose
precondition nothing establishes; no diff base that excludes uncommitted work; no
persuasion register; no silently-failing hook; no `PreToolUse` deny as a security
control; no trust in a declared hook timeout; no percentage without a control
arm; no light path without a runtime; no roster change that stops at the roster.

And one addition from §6.3: **no bundled MCP servers, ever** — it is the only
plugin component the harness lets invalidate the prompt cache.

## 10. Open questions, and what could not be verified

### 10.1 The genuine tensions

These are unresolved, and the document does not pretend otherwise.

| Tension | One side | Other side |
|---|---|---|
| **Does length matter?** | Anthropic deleted 80% of Claude Code's system prompt with "no measurable loss"; Gemini 3 says "be concise" | The largest experiment found **no effect of size**, and the **25-line file scored lowest**; OpenAI's GPT-5.2 guide advocates long structured constraint blocks and never warns about length |
| **Does repetition help?** | Anthropic's stated shift is "repetition → concise descriptions" | Prompt repetition won **47 of 70** benchmark-model combinations with zero losses; and Anthropic's *own* Opus 5 page says to pair a rule with a reminder near the end |
| **Should a subagent verify?** | Opus 5 guidance: *"do not use subagents to verify or double-check your own work"* | Four measurements say clean-context verification works (§5.5) |
| **Is delegation worth it?** | Nature MI: every multi-agent arm lost on SWE-bench Verified; BOAD's optimum is two agents | Cognition reversed its own position; context collapses 96% → 14.7% from 8K to 256K, which is what isolation buys back |
| **Is the ceiling falling?** | Three independent measurements — cross-component interference vanishes on Claude Haiku; instruction-compiler gains are +11pp weak, ~0 strong; Harness-Bench finds lower cross-harness variance on stronger backends | omc-slim targets Opus 5, the strongest tier, where all three say the layer matters least |

The last row is the strategic one. **The value of any prompt layer appears to
decay with each model generation**, which means omc-slim's numbers need
re-measuring per model release, not just per plugin release.

### 10.2 omc-slim's own reinforcement result is within noise

`PROVENANCE.md` treats superpowers' 8/10 → 5/10 compliance result as load-bearing
evidence for relocating rather than deleting an argument. At n=10 per arm the 95%
confidence interval on a 0.8 proportion runs roughly **0.44 to 0.97** — the two
arms overlap heavily, and the eval-randomness literature puts σ above 1.5pp even
at temperature 0. **A 3/10 shift is not a detectable effect.** It should be a
hypothesis, not a result.

Two further reasons to doubt it transfers. The prompt-repetition mechanism that
would explain it is **neutral on reasoning models** (5 wins, 1 loss, 22 neutral),
and Opus 5 is one. And the *relocation* half of the finding is contradicted by
the n=1,650 study that found no position effect at all.

**But there is a reading that survives all of it, and it favours omc-slim.** The
factorial study manipulated size, position, architecture and cross-file
contradiction. It did not manipulate **content quality**. The superpowers result
deleted *rebuttals* — arguments that resolve a rule against a competing pull —
which is a content change, not a placement change. Instruction-stacking collapse
identifies **conflict** as the mechanism, and a rebuttal is precisely what
resolves a conflict.

So the defensible version of the rule is not "relocate rather than delete because
position matters". It is **"a rule that has a competing reading needs the
argument that settles it"** — which is a claim about conflict, the one mechanism
the literature does support. `REINFORCEMENT.tsv` should be justified that way.

No controlled 2026 experiment on whether a rationale attached to a rule changes
compliance was found in this sweep. This remains omc-slim's own unique data
point, and it should be labelled as such rather than cited as settled.

It is also cheap to settle: it is a single binary compliance check, so 100 trials
per arm is affordable and would answer it properly.

### 10.3 Unverified, and one claim believed wrong

- **Anthropic's multi-agent research system post** — the widely-cited 90.2%
  improvement and ~15× token multiplier. One lane reached it, two did not. The
  one that did records the deflating detail: on BrowseComp, **token usage alone
  explains 80% of the variance**, which makes the headline substantially a
  compute effect. It is also a *research* workload, and Anthropic itself writes
  that *"most coding tasks involve fewer truly parallelizable tasks than
  research."*
- **`anthropic.com/news/advanced-tool-use` returns 404.** The "85% token
  reduction" for Tool Search is from secondary coverage. No accuracy delta has
  been published for it — only token savings.
- **Believed wrong:** a widely circulating "Cursor 93% vs Claude Code 77% on
  Terminal-Bench 2.0" harness claim. 93% exceeds every audited TB 2.0 entry (max
  78.4%) and it traces to a personal blog. Do not cite it.
- **No controlled study of one-question-at-a-time versus batched clarification**
  was found, despite it being a load-bearing rule in `deep-interview`.
- **No controlled study of whether a rationale attached to a rule changes
  compliance** was found. That is the exact mechanism `REINFORCEMENT.tsv` is
  built to protect.
- **The self-correction figures in §4.2** — 342 worsened, 233 improved, net
  −2.1%, 69.6% false problem identification — **are not in either cited source**.
  arXiv:2310.01798 is qualitative; arXiv:2510.12171 is MatSciBench, a
  materials-science benchmark that covers self-correction but confirms none of
  the three. Struck there, recorded here, and not relied on anywhere else.
- SWE-bench solution-leakage figures disagree across studies (33.5% versus
  60.8%) and were not reconciled.
- **No Claude Code or Cursor destructive-action postmortem was found.** Do not
  assert one exists. The two incidents usually cited are Replit's (no formal
  postmortem published, only CEO posts) and Gemini CLI's (retracted by the
  reporter — see §7.10).
- **No study measures regressions caused specifically by LLM code *removal*.**
  The adjacent literature is about reward hacking on tests, which is a different
  question.
- The measurements in §7.10 are **single-repo and single-run**. The compression
  ratios are real but repo-shape-dependent: `ast-grep outline`'s 167× on
  class-based TypeScript collapses to three useless lines on prototype-assignment
  CommonJS.
- The Nature MI SWE-bench cells are **n=20 with ±20pp bootstrap confidence
  intervals**; the ~45% threshold is a validated *selection rule*, not a scaling
  law, and its leave-one-domain-out R² is −2.09 — it ranks architectures within a
  domain and does not predict absolute performance across domains.

Two lanes delivered a report, then delivered a second version retracting part of
the first.

The superpowers lane's first delivery presented specific measurements of

That is [superpowers#1749](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/issues/1749) —

## 11. What practitioners actually did in 2026

The community lane found something the papers cannot supply: people who built a
large layer, measured it, and then deleted most of it — with commit hashes.

### 11.1 The strongest single data point in this document

**Nick Nisi, WorkOS.** Talk: *"How I deleted 95% of my agent skills and got
better results"*
([AI Engineer, 2026-05-30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vy7o1g2iHY8)).
Receipt: [`workos/skills` commit `059fc5d`](https://github.com/workos/skills/commit/059fc5deda7d7458ca2fc4fa687c32a924083eb3),
2026-03-07 — **+556 / −10,183 across 91 files** (corrected 2026-08-26 against the
GitHub API; this document first published +553).

| Result | Value |
|---|---|
| Eval runtime | 68 min → **6 min** |
| Worst single skill | **−20%** — with it a task passed 77% of the time, without it **97%** |
| Honest caveat, from his own commit message | The lean version **initially regressed −6.5%** and he had to add three specific traps back |

He found the −20% skill only because he A/B'd **every** skill. That is the
experiment omc-slim does not have and `claude plugin eval --ablation
with-without` would give it (§6.1).

**The less-quoted half matters more for this plugin.** He moved multi-step flow
control *out of SKILL.md prose and into a TypeScript state machine* with gates,
because prose orchestration *"worked most of the time and failed unpredictably
the rest — it would skip the verifier, or retry four times when the cap was
one."* He SHA-256-hashes test output so an agent cannot fake a pass with `touch`.
His formulation: **"trust is a pass rate, a hash, a delta score. Not a
feeling."**

That is `review`'s re-review cap and `deepwork`'s stage gates, both of which are
prose in omc-slim today.

### 11.2 Two independent deletion audits, same week

Both 2026-07-26, neither aware of the other.

- **Koutian Wu**: 174 skills, **124 never invoked once**. Skill *listing* cost
  ~13,800 tokens against a budget of roughly 1% of the window; disabling the
  unused ones took it to ~5,700. His keep/cut rule is the sharpest formulation
  found anywhere: **does the skill compensate for something the model *cannot*
  do, or for something it merely could not do *yet*?** Access, cross-model
  critique, repo conventions and claim-verification stay. Procedures the model
  can now infer go. And the warning: *"A stale skill is not neutral. It is a
  fixed instruction competing with the model's own judgment, and it wins, because
  I wrote it as an instruction."*
- **Joe Cotellese**: 154 markdown files, ~40,000 lines; deleted **8,404 lines
  across 41 files**. The findings are worse than staleness — they are
  contradiction. His `swift-architect` skill recommended a wrapper his own
  standards document banned in capitals. His **code reviewer was instructed to
  verify state management using one of the wrappers it existed to flag.** Two
  React Native skills carried 112 references to Recoil, archived by Meta on
  2025-01-01, still shipping as the house default.

Cotellese's conclusion is the one to carry into every future omc-slim release:

> "The model getting better is what made the bloat safe to delete. Everything
> else moving is what made the contradictions dangerous. Those are different
> problems and only one of them is about token count."

And: **"Config doesn't crash."** It just quietly makes the tool worse, and the
tool sounds equally confident either way.

### 11.3 A mature orchestrator was destroyed by a model release

**Steve Yegge's Gas Town died.** In his own words
([The Shape of Things to Come](https://yegge.ai/essays/the-shape-of-things-to-come/)):

> "Gas Town was intended to be reusable, but I only ever wound up using it to
> build itself. Gas Town fell apart at the seams with Opus 4.7. Up through 4.6 it
> was working brilliantly. With 4.7 we saw the introduction of the 'just two more
> things' tic, which prevented Opus from ever converging… Gas Town effectively
> burned down."

This is the single strongest argument for a small surface area, and it comes from
the ecosystem's loudest maximalist. It compounds §4.6 — the baseline moves — and
§10.1 — the ceiling falls. **A layer's exposure to a model release scales with
how much of it is load-bearing.**

Thorsten Ball's version of the same lesson: *"the only winning move now is to
accept that the board will be flipped at random intervals."*

### 11.4 Anthropic's own harness is walking back subagents

Two community findings that bear directly on omc-slim's architecture:

- Claude Code ships **a hardcoded instruction telling Opus 5 not to use
  subagents** ([r/ClaudeCode](https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeCode/comments/1v6y5q2/claude_code_has_a_hardcoded_instruction_telling/),
  [HN 49056022](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49056022)). This session's own
  system prompt carries it.
- **Simon Willison's subagent verdict** is a direct warning to any subagent-heavy
  plugin: *"While it can be tempting to go overboard breaking up tasks across
  dozens of different specialist subagents… the main value of subagents is in
  preserving that valuable root context and managing token-heavy operations. Your
  root coding agent is perfectly capable of debugging or reviewing its own output
  provided it has the tokens to spare."*

His current routing pattern is worth stealing verbatim as a memory rather than a
plugin: *"For all coding tasks use your judgement to decide an appropriate lower
power model and run that in a subagent"* — implementation to cheaper tiers,
judgment and synthesis in the main loop.

### 11.5 A measured result against a skill omc-slim does not ship, and should not

**Birgitta Böckeler, Thoughtworks** — the best-run experiment of the year
([TDD inside the agent loop](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/tdd-in-the-agent-loop.html),
with a [committed data repo](https://github.com/birgitta410/tdd-comparisons/)):

> "there was no clearly discernable difference based on TDD workflow versus no
> TDD workflow. On the contrary, more than once Opus ranked the non-TDD workflow
> solutions slightly higher."

Token cost of the TDD arm: **8.5× (small), 2.96× (medium), 4.89× (large)**. Her
mechanism finding is the interesting part — the non-TDD runs *"created the full
design (architecture, data types, edge cases, contracts) before writing any code
or tests, rather than working through it one requirement at a time."*

omc-slim's `fixer` mandates a reproduction test before a bug fix, which is a
narrower and better-supported claim than TDD-as-workflow. But
`verification-planning` should not drift toward prescribing TDD, and this is the
citation for why.

Her **sensors** framing is the constructive half: deterministic tools first
(typecheck, semgrep, dependency-cruiser, coverage, incremental mutation testing,
gitleaks), LLM interpretation second. Two reusable details — mutation output is a
huge JSON file, so she generated a query script *so the agent would not clog its
own context*; and **coverage lies**, with one case at 100% statements, zero unit
tests, and 13 surviving mutants.

### 11.6 The cheapest win found anywhere

`{"env":{"ENABLE_TOOL_SEARCH":"true"}}` took one user's `/context` from **45k to
15.5k tokens**
([r/ClaudeCode, 926-session audit](https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeCode/comments/1sd8t5u/anthropic_isnt_the_only_reason_youre_hitting/)).

That is a user setting, not a plugin change — which makes it documentation, not
a feature. It belongs in the README beside the `subagentPromptCacheTtl` note
(§6.2), because a delegation-first plugin's users are exactly the people paying
that tax.

### 11.7 The critique omc-slim has to be able to answer

The most-quoted comment on the plugin ecosystem in 2026, **@sandrello**
([HN 48289950](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48289950), 451 points):

> "Generally, and more so with paid products, one should expect to get something
> ready to be used… Instead this is basically saying that the product is not much
> more than an empty box, and that it is your responsibility to augment it with
> third-party plugins and markdown texts that make it finally useful… No matter
> the problem you're facing, the blame is definitely on you, the user, for not
> setting up the tool in the right way."

And **@noodletheworld**, in February, correctly predicting the whole year:

> "Is it just me, or do skills seem enormously similar to MCP? …MCP is perfectly
> fine when you run your own locally. It's bad when you install some arbitrary
> MCP from some random person. It fails when you have too many installed. Same
> for skills."

The honest answer omc-slim can give is not "we are better." It is: **six agents,
six skills, one hook, no MCP servers, nothing written to your repo, every claim
dated, and a list of what we could not verify.** That is a different product
category from a 900-skill marketplace, and this document is part of the evidence
for it.

### 11.8 The experiment nobody has run

Across three separate HN threads in 2026, practitioners asked for the same
controlled comparison and nobody produced it. **@ritsukai**, verbatim:

> "whether they would do better than just markdown on its own isn't really
> settled. Would be interesting to see same tasks, same agent, same context
> budget, skill vs the same content pasted into AGENTS.md."

**No published controlled experiment compares a skill library against the same
content as plain prose at equal context budget.** If omc-slim ran that on its own
thirteen components it would be the first public result of its kind — and, given
the Nisi and Böckeler findings, **the outcome is genuinely not predictable in
advance.**

That is the most interesting thing this project could do next, and it costs one
`claude plugin eval` suite.

### 11.9 A control-armed evaluation exists, and it names the winning mechanism

omc-slim refused superpowers' own behavioural eval for having **no control arm**
([`PROVENANCE.md`](./PROVENANCE.md)). That refusal was correct. But a
third-party, MIT-licensed, control-armed, **blind-judged twice** evaluation now
exists, with public data files:
[luobosibing2/superpowers-workflow-evaluation](https://github.com/luobosibing2/superpowers-workflow-evaluation)
(created 2026-08-02 — corrected 2026-08-26; this document first published
2026-08-14). Five methods, 15 runs, one frozen underspecified task, same model,
effort, baseline and rubric.

| Method | Blind score | Dedup tokens | Wall clock |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **`without` — native Codex, the control arm** | 81.67 | 2.08M | 8:36 |
| Slim (4 skills) | 83.17 | ~2.27M | ~9:03 |
| **Slim + requirement-approval loop** | **97.67** | ~2.87M | ~11:20 |
| Slim + requirement + review loops | **99.50** | ~4.88M | ~20:37 |
| Full Superpowers (14 skills) | 99.00 | **15.87M** | **40:24** |

Three results, each landing directly on omc-slim's design.

**1. Skills alone bought almost nothing: +1.50 points over bare Codex.** Within
noise, and consistent with every study in §2.5.

**2. The approval gate was the entire win — +14.50 points for +0.60M tokens and
two minutes.** The author's own summary: *"The largest observed quality jump was
between Slim and the requirement loop."*

That is the single highest-leverage mechanism measured anywhere in this
document, and **omc-slim already has it** — `deep-interview` writes a spec and
**STOPS for approval before any code**. The finding is not that it should be
added. It is that it should be **protected and generalised**, and that it is
currently one of thirteen components rather than the centrepiece.

It also reframes §7.9's caveat. The *one-question-at-a-time* rule has no
controlled evidence behind it, but the **approval gate it terminates in** now
does.

**3. Full Superpowers did not beat a four-skill slim plus two loops** — 99.00
against 99.50, while spending **7.6× the control arm's tokens and 4.7× its wall
clock**. And the breakdown of where that went is the fan-out tax, measured:
**cached input was 95.3% of the token delta**, 37.9% of it coordination. Heavy
context *transport*, not heavy thinking.

**The fourth result is the one for `verification-planning`.** Across all 15 runs:

> "All 15 focused test logs exited zero, yet strict blind-judge `Verified` labels
> were 0/6, 0/6, 0/6, 4/6, and 3/6 across the five methods."

Every suite went green. Independent blind judging verified as few as **zero of
six**. That is §7.7's vacuous-fixture finding reproduced at experimental scale,
and it is the strongest available argument for the revert-and-rerun ritual and
the positive control.

**Limits, as the author states them and I repeat rather than launder:** cohorts
ran at different times, so this is not one contemporaneous five-arm
randomisation; the canonical loop run is a user-approved post-hoc rerun; one full
run hit the token cap. Descriptive, not causal. It is still better evidence than
anything upstream publishes.

**A cross-workflow panel exists too** — 5 workflows × 8 tasks × 3 replicates =
120 runs, 240 blind scores
([cyijun/workflow-arena](https://github.com/cyijun/workflow-arena)). **Grill Me
scored 92.14 against Full Superpowers' 89.64, at 18% of the tokens and 38% of the
wall clock.** But it has **no concurrent bare arm**, uses a different model, and
does not publish raw sessions — so it is cross-task context and must not be
combined with the table above.

One incidental finding from it is a defect class worth naming: MatrixSpec's 12
protocol failures came from *"nine cases of the reviewer modifying product
files."* **A reviewer that writes is a defect.** omc-slim avoids it structurally
— `oracle` and every dispatched `review` lane are read-only — and that is worth
stating as a deliberate property rather than an accident.

### 11.10 omc-slim is the median of the informed reaction, not an outlier

**Verified here** by direct API call: `tim-hub/superpowers-slim` and
`luobosibing2/superpowers-slim` both exist (0 stars each, MIT).
`obra/superpowers-slim` returns 404 — neither fork is upstream's.

| | luobosibing2 | tim-hub | **omc-slim** | superpowers v6.3.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skills | 4 | 9 | **6** | 14 |
| Bytes under skills | 8,413 | 52,113 | **~140,316** (skills + agents) | 369,641 |
| Hook | 1 tiny path adapter | **none** | **1, on `SubagentStop`** | 1, on `SessionStart` |
| Harnesses targeted | 1 | 1 | **1** | 11 |

Both forks were created within a day of each other, 2026-08-02 and 2026-08-03 —
the "this is too heavy" reaction crystallised across the community at one moment.
There is even a [patch kit](https://github.com/BB-84C/omo-slim-superpowers-patch-kit)
for making superpowers coexist with oh-my-opencode-slim, which means the friction
is real enough that people ship compatibility layers for it.

**omc-slim sits between the two forks in philosophy and above both in bytes.**
Combined with §2.3 — its always-on surface measured *larger* than upstream's —
the honest position is that omc-slim is disciplined about *surface* and not yet
disciplined about *body*.

**One concrete borrow from tim-hub**, and it is the cheapest way to fix that:
**`SKILL.md` carries workflow steps only, with depth behind sibling-file
pointers.** `review` already does this with `checklists.md` and
`performance.md`. `deepwork` at 332 lines and `simplify` at 258 do not.

And tim-hub's reasoning is better sourced than my earlier evidence for the same
conclusion. It cites Anthropic directly:

> "Overall, we found that we were **overconstraining Claude Code**, both through
> our system prompt and in our CLAUDE.md files **and skills**."

with the mechanism being wasted deliberation rather than disobedience — *"Claude
must think more carefully about these overlapping and conflicting messages before
deciding what to do."* That is a **first-party citation for the refusal of the
persuasion register**, stronger than the two bug reports in §4.1 item 9.
